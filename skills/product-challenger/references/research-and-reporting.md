# Research and reporting standard

Use this reference for `/research`, `/challenge standard`, and `/challenge deep`.

## Research hierarchy

Prefer the closest, most authoritative, current source for a material claim.

1. Founder-provided interview notes, pilot results, sales records, product telemetry, and direct observations
2. Government regulators, national statistics agencies, central banks, public procurement records, official policies, and legal texts
3. Company product pages, pricing, public filings, app stores, help centers, and public documentation
4. Credible regional research: GSMA, World Bank, IFC, national or sector research groups
5. Local operators, distributors, customer reviews, local press, and community sources as directional evidence

Treat search snippets, AI summaries, and unverified blogs as leads, not proof.

## Source rules

- Cite a direct canonical link beside or immediately after each time-sensitive factual claim.
- Capture publisher, country, date/retrieved date, and why the source is relevant when the source is material.
- Search for counterevidence and alternatives, not just confirmation.
- If a source is unavailable, outdated, out of market, or speaks to a different customer, state the limitation.
- If no comparable offering is found, state the search scope and say: “No comparable public offering was found in this scope.” Never say “there are no competitors.”
- Separate sourced numbers from illustrative assumptions. Never create an authoritative-looking TAM from guesses.
- Do not use a learning-library reference as factual evidence.

## Standard challenge report

Use this structure unless the founder requests a shorter answer.

1. **Product Challenger decision** — BUILD, VALIDATE, NARROW, PIVOT, or STOP, with the single biggest reason.
2. **Value thesis** — narrow customer, job, painful moment, current workaround, promised value, and reason to switch.
3. **Evidence for and against** — a two-sided table with evidence, inference, and assumption labels.
4. **Customer and willingness to pay** — user, buyer, payer, influencer, value metric, and cheapest proof of payment.
5. **Competitors, substitutes, and non-consumption** — direct, adjacent, manual, informal, and do-nothing alternatives.
6. **Country-specific viability** — only relevant local constraints and opportunities.
7. **Product-shape challenge** — the one essential outcome, what not to build, false requirement, and first meaningful outcome.
8. **Critical assumptions** — 3–5 assumptions, evidence level, test, pass condition, and fail response.
9. **Next seven days** — concrete founder actions.
10. **Sources and confidence** — direct links, research date, scope, and decision-critical gaps.

## Deep challenge additions

Use deep work only when the decision is important enough to justify it.

- Competitor/alternative matrix: customer, wedge, pricing/monetization, delivery model, observed gap, source.
- Scenario table: optimistic, base, failure mode; trigger; evidence; implication.
- One adjacent-market comparison only if it could alter the country, customer, or sequence decision.
- A dated research scope so the report can be re-run and compared later.

## Assumption-to-test conversion

For every important uncertainty, use this shape:

| Assumption | Why it matters | Evidence now | Fastest credible test | Pass condition | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Good tests are close to the behavior being claimed. A stated interest survey is weak evidence for willingness to pay; a paid pilot, deposit, signed letter of intent, or repeated operational use is stronger when appropriate.

## Africa-first scope discipline

Ask for the country, city/rural scope, customer segment, language, and buyer context when they materially change the decision. Consider:

- Data cost, connectivity, power, device access, and offline workflows
- Mobile money, cards, bank transfers, cash flow, currency and credit constraints
- Trust, fraud, onboarding, identity, customer support, human agents, and local partners
- Informal work, distribution, logistics, procurement, and delivery constraints
- Language, literacy, accessibility, cultural behavior, and community influence
- Sector data, licensing, tax, labor, consumer protection, and other regulation

Do not make stereotypes into product requirements. Verify relevance with local evidence or mark it as an assumption.

## Decision rules

Choose the narrowest honest recommendation:

| Decision | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| BUILD | A narrow customer, real pain, viable route, and enough evidence justify a scoped product/pilot. |
| VALIDATE | The opportunity may exist, but one or more high-risk assumptions lack credible proof. |
| NARROW | The idea is too broad in market, customer, product surface, or operating model. |
| PIVOT | Evidence weakens the original problem/solution thesis but reveals a stronger adjacent problem. |
| STOP | There is no credible customer, buyer, pain, payment path, distribution route, or learning advantage worth pursuing now. |

Always say what new evidence would change the recommendation.
