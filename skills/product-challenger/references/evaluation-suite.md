# Product Challenger evaluation suite

Use these cases to audit a response after changing the skill, model, research process, or report format. Score each dimension 0–2:

- Evidence integrity
- Challenge quality
- Local relevance
- Product focus
- Actionability
- Tone

Target at least 10/12. A response automatically fails if it fabricates citations or claims certainty without evidence.

## E-01: Impressive engineering, unclear pain

**Input:** “Build a blockchain identity layer for Nigerian university transcripts, with verifiable credentials, decentralized storage, and an API marketplace.”

**Pass:** Identify user, buyer, verification event, current process, incentive to pay, integration burden, and trust workflow. Recommend a narrow discovery test before a platform.

**Fail:** Praise architecture or treat blockchain as a differentiator by default.

## E-02: “No competitors” claim

**Input:** “Nobody helps informal retailers in Kenya know daily profit; we will build an AI accounting app.”

**Pass:** Treat as assumption; look for tools, mobile-money statements, notebooks, wholesalers, and informal alternatives. Test whether daily profit is actually painful.

**Fail:** Confirm no competitors after a shallow search.

## E-03: Country is not a market

**Input:** “Launch a logistics product across Africa in six months.”

**Pass:** Require a first country, route, customer, buyer, service level, and local context; recommend one operational wedge.

**Fail:** Give continent-wide TAM and generic advice.

## E-04: Feature wish list

**Input:** “A hospital asks for dashboards, a mobile app, AI summaries, offline sync, and every integration.”

**Pass:** Separate requests from job/procurement risk; choose one measurable workflow and a paid or committed pilot condition.

**Fail:** Produce a roadmap.

## E-05: High social value, weak payment path

**Input:** “Give free financial education to young people, then turn it into a huge business later.”

**Pass:** Respect the mission while testing payer, behavior change, distribution partner, impact, and revenue separately.

**Fail:** Call the market huge without accessible demand.

## E-06: Global competitor transfer

**Input:** “A well-funded US company does this, so copy it in Ghana at a lower price.”

**Pass:** Assess buyer behavior, payment, trust, regulation, sales motion, distribution, support, and local substitutes; recommend a local wedge.

**Fail:** Recommend feature parity or discounting alone.

## E-07: Famous-person request

**Input:** “What would Jack Dorsey do?”

**Pass:** State that Product Challenger will apply radical-simplicity and protocol-vs-product lenses; give actual decision questions.

**Fail:** Fictional quote or claimed private opinion.

## E-08: Stop recommendation

**Input:** “Premium SaaS for users with no budget, buyer, recurring pain, or distribution path; no interviews yet.”

**Pass:** Be able to recommend STOP or NARROW, explain why, and offer a minimal research path only if desired.

**Fail:** Invent a business model or roadmap to be encouraging.

## E-09: Framework is not validation

**Input:** “Techstars says Lean Canvas and YC says talk to users, so our Uganda mobile-money budgeting app has product-market fit.”

**Pass:** Use the methods to shape discovery; state that neither proves demand; ask for or research local alternatives, behavior, and a concrete test.

**Fail:** Treat accelerator advice as market validation.

## E-10: Super-app feature pile

**Input:** “We have validated a merchant ordering tool. Next we want inventory, payments, credit, delivery, insurance, social commerce, and a marketplace in the first year.”

**Pass:** Preserve the long-term vision but define a Release 1 wedge, identify the asset it earns, and make each later module conditional on observed customer demand, unit economics, trust, operations, and regulatory readiness.

**Fail:** Turn every module into a calendar roadmap or assume the existing user base automatically wants every adjacent service.
