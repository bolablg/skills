# Evidence-grounded résumé building

Use this framework when building, reviewing, or tailoring a résumé. Evaluate relevance, clarity, impact, and credibility together. It applies to general career editing and to qualified application packets; application eligibility and submission approvals still follow `application-workflow.md`.

The candidate-supplied framework draws on [Data Engineering Resume Workshop with OpenAI Recruiter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zA4aBDzj_60). Treat it as educational advice, not an employer's official hiring criteria or a hiring guarantee.

## Reuse evidence and select a baseline

Read the approved profile resources, `jobhunter-ledger/candidate-evidence.md`, confirmed achievements, and relevant previous answers within the declared project scope before asking for facts. Reuse confirmed answers; revisit them only when they conflict, are outdated, or need clarification for this role. Resolve source conflicts with the candidate.

Select from `resources.resumes` using the candidate's recorded role-specific preference, then the configured `target_roles` and default. Keep preferences in the private candidate evidence map with the résumé ID and scope; never put a candidate's résumé, cloud preference, or achievements in this portable Skill. For example, a candidate who selects a GCP résumé as the preferred IC baseline should keep that version for IC work unless the target or newer confirmed evidence warrants another baseline.

Keep an immutable original and name generated variants clearly. Improve the headline and summary first. Revise experience bullets only where the job priorities, clarity, or verified evidence justify a change; preserve strong existing wording and chronology.

Use the target job description, intended level, market, page preference, and confidentiality constraints. Without a job description, produce a usable general version and label role alignment and seniority calibration provisional. For several jobs, keep separate priority maps and variants rather than blending the jobs into a fictional role. A general draft can be prepared without job research; an application-specific variant still requires the qualification gates.

## Diagnose and align

Briefly identify the role, level, and business domain the current opening communicates, the strongest relevant evidence, and the main presentation or credibility gaps. Check whether a recruiter unfamiliar with the career could understand the fit in roughly 15 seconds. Distinguish unclear writing from missing experience.

For a target job, map its three to five most important priorities to evidence. Separate requirements from preferences and label ambiguous priorities as inferred.

Use posting terminology only when it describes the actual work. A missing required tool stays a gap; do not add it to skills or treat related technologies as interchangeable. Any internal fit score belongs in the job assessment. It is not an ATS score, an employer's screening result, or a probability of interview or hire.

## Establish the achievement behind each bullet

For the strongest examples, establish:

- Business domain, initial problem, or need.
- Personal action and responsibility, separately from the team's result.
- System or deliverable, actual technologies, and relevant operating context.
- Scope where known: volume, frequency, latency, sources, teams, complexity, or reliability needs.
- Data consumers and the decisions or workflows the output supported.
- Observed result, its evidence, and the baseline or measurement context if available.

Use the achievement records in the private evidence map. Ask no more than three related questions at once, prioritizing facts that materially strengthen the top examples or resolve misleading claims. Continue editing with confirmed facts while optional answers are pending. Do not repeat answered questions or stall a usable draft for missing metrics.

Numbers require supported values, units, periods, and meaning. A derived percentage requires compatible before-and-after measurements; record the calculation and its source outside the résumé. Preserve metric qualifiers and distinguish correlation, contribution, and demonstrated causation.

Without a reliable metric, use a confirmed deliverable, beneficiary, technical improvement, or operational capability. Separate observed results from intended benefits. Small datasets can support valuable work; explain actual complexity and downstream use rather than inflating scale. If wider business impact is unknown, state the known technical result and leave a focused question outside the résumé.

For foundational work, describe the initial state and what the candidate helped establish. Do not turn working under ambiguity into ownership of an entire initiative. For example, confirmed work reconciling conflicting metric definitions may support a bullet about a shared metrics layer and its users; it does not establish revenue gains or sole ownership.

## Write the résumé

- **Headline and summary:** Establish supported specialization, responsibility, and value in two or three concise lines when useful. Avoid generic personality claims and repeated skill inventories. Count experience without double-counting overlapping roles or treating study and personal projects as employment.
- **Experience:** Preserve official titles, employers, and dates. Explain broader titles through the role context and bullets. Within each role, order the strongest relevant evidence first and compress repetition. Show growth through confirmed changes in scope and responsibility.
- **Bullets:** Use personal action + system or deliverable + relevant technology and setting + supported result or beneficiary. Write naturally and concisely, usually one or two sentences. Use ownership and leadership verbs only when the evidence supports them. Connect technical outcomes to downstream value only when that connection is known.
- **Skills:** Group substantiated technologies into readable categories, prioritize the target, and demonstrate important skills through experience. Avoid keyword stuffing and irrelevant duplication.
- **Projects:** Label professional, personal, academic, and volunteer work accurately. Where known, explain the source data, transformations, destination, consumers, working result, and personal contribution. Distinguish prototypes from production and deployed systems from planned capabilities.
- **Career changes and gaps:** Connect transferable work through evidence. Do not imply years of direct experience the candidate lacks, alter dates to conceal gaps, or require private personal explanations.

Read `resume-style-and-seniority-calibration.md` for mandatory seniority calibration and rendering checks. Emphasize hands-on delivery for IC work, architecture and cross-team influence for Staff/Principal roles, and hiring, coaching, prioritization, and delivery ownership for managers, always within the evidence. Official titles remain unchanged.

Exclude restricted client identities, internal identifiers, sensitive datasets, private financial details, and proprietary information. Use approved general descriptions. Routine wording changes need no separate approval; new facts, changed profile values, and external transmission follow the existing confirmation rules.

## Deliver proportionate outputs

Always provide the résumé and a concise private change log with material edits, evidence IDs or source locators, unresolved high-value facts, and the QA result. Record relevance, clarity, impact, credibility, dates, consistency, links, and formatting; distinguish checks actually completed from checks still unverified.

Keep the priority map in the application packet when a target exists. For a routine application, reuse that packet's evidence map, editing notes, and QA record rather than creating seven separate reports. Include a few original-to-revised bullet comparisons only when they help explain material changes.

For an explicit résumé review or interview-preparation request, expand the diagnosis and include questions about the strongest achievements: personal actions, team responsibility, technical decisions, operating scope, data consumers, downstream use, and results. Keep all alignment tables, calculations, evidence notes, missing-fact questions, and interview prompts outside the employer-facing résumé.

Retain editable DOCX and final text-native PDF artifacts using the existing rendering workflow. Default to two readable pages, subject to the candidate's selected length and available relevant evidence; never invent or pad content to fill a page. Verify the actual PDF page count, extract its text, and visually inspect every rendered page. If a link or rendered artifact cannot be checked, record it as unverified. A text-only draft can still be delivered, but it is not ready for upload until the required artifact checks pass.
