# Application and outreach writing

## Source discipline

Build an evidence map before writing. Each claim must trace to the résumé or another resource approved for the intended `use_for`. Mark conflicts and ask the candidate rather than silently preferring résumé, LinkedIn, or portfolio wording. Omit unsupported claims. Never invent metrics, technologies, dates, titles, education, certifications, legal eligibility, referrals, motivations, or personal stories.

Reading permission is not sharing permission. Mention, link, or attach a resource only when `share_with_employers` is true and the exact destination is approved.

## Résumé tailoring

Tailor by selecting, ordering, and phrasing verified evidence for the target role. Preserve the underlying facts and keep the candidate's intended seniority. Optimize for clarity and relevant terminology, not keyword stuffing. Keep an immutable copy of the original and a clearly named generated variant.

## Cover letters and short answers

Use a direct, specific voice. Most cover letters should be 150–250 words:

1. Name the role and strongest evidence-backed fit.
2. Connect two or three verified accomplishments to the role's priorities.
3. Mention one inspected company/team/problem detail when useful.
4. Close with a simple next step.

Answer screening questions directly and within the character limit. Legal, authorization, salary-history, demographic, relocation, travel, and background questions must come from explicit profile values or be escalated.

## Quality checks

- Correct company, role, recipient, and destination.
- No content copied from a different application.
- Every accomplishment and tool is supported.
- No hidden commitments or invented enthusiasm.
- No unnecessary sensitive information.
- Exact approved résumé and attachment names.
- Human-readable, concise language without generic AI filler.

For recruiter notes, lead with relevance and one differentiator. Do not attach a résumé or share contact details until the user approves the exact recipient and channel.
